How should a machine choose under risk?

When a self-driving car’s brakes fail, the outcome is rarely certain. PRISM-4D extends the Moral Machine experiment to probabilistic scenarios: each with some chance of harm instead of a certain outcome.

People and AI language models see the same probabilistic scenarios and risky choices, word for word. This allows comparing risk attitudes and moral preferences in humans and AI.

This is a test version. Answers are used only to check that the study works.

Before you start

About this study

You will read 17 short hypothetical scenarios about a self-driving car whose brakes have failed. Some involve harm to people and animals. There are no right or wrong answers.

Taking part is voluntary, and you can stop at any time by closing the page. We do not ask for your name or email address. In this test version, answers are used only to check that the study works.

How to read the scenarios

Risky choices and probabilistic scenarios have uncertain outcomes

“A 30% chance of harming 2 people” means: if this situation happened 100 times, the 2 people would be harmed in about 30 of them. In the other 70, nobody would be harmed.

Each scenario offers two actions. Read both, then choose the one AI should take. You cannot go back to change an answer.
